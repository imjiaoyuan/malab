Research

Our group aims to understand how plant sex, adaptation, and diversity are encoded and evolve in genomes. We use poplars and willows (Salicaceae) as our main system, integrating genome, epigenome, transcriptome, and single-cell data with population genetics and molecular experiments.

Current interests include the origin and evolution of sex chromosomes, the genomic basis of drought, salinity, and cold tolerance, the processes of speciation and diversification, and the regulatory roles of chromatin and genome structure.

By connecting broad evolutionary patterns with molecular mechanisms, we hope to understand the origins of plant diversity and inform tree improvement. Prospective students and postdocs are welcome to contact us.
